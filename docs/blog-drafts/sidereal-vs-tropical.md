# Sidereal vs Tropical Zodiac: Why Your Western Horoscope and Vedic Kundli Don't Match

Someone reads their Western horoscope for years as a Leo, has a kundli made in India, and is told they're a Cancer. Nothing has gone wrong. Both readings are describing the same sky, measured against two different starting points, and the gap between those starting points is now about 24 degrees.

That gap has a name, an exact value, and a reason. It's worth understanding, because it decides which of the two charts your dasha dates and your mangal dosha status can legitimately be calculated from.

## What the two zodiacs actually measure

Both divide the sky into twelve equal thirty-degree segments. They disagree about where segment one begins.

The tropical zodiac pins 0 degrees Aries to the spring equinox, the moment the Sun crosses the celestial equator heading north. It's tied to the seasons, which is why tropical Aries always starts around 21 March regardless of what the stars are doing.

The sidereal zodiac pins 0 degrees Aries to a fixed point among the stars. The word comes from sidus, Latin for star. Segment one begins where it began when the system was set down, and it stays there.

## Why they've drifted apart

Because the equinox moves. The Earth's axis wobbles like a slow spinning top, completing one full circuit in roughly 25,800 years, so the equinox point creeps backwards through the constellations at about one degree every 72 years. Astronomers call it the precession of the equinoxes.

The two zodiacs agreed around the fifth century. They've been separating ever since, and in 2026 the difference is 24 degrees and 13 minutes.

Here's what that means in practice: the constellation the Sun is actually in front of on your birthday is nearly a full sign behind the tropical sign your Western horoscope assigns you.

## What it does to your Sun sign

Most people move back one sign. A Sun at 12 degrees tropical Leo sits at 17 degrees sidereal Cancer. A Sun at 3 degrees tropical Sagittarius lands at 9 degrees sidereal Scorpio.

The exception is worth knowing. If you were born in roughly the last six days of a tropical sign, subtracting 24 degrees leaves you inside the same sign, so nothing changes. That's why some people find their Vedic and Western signs identical and assume one of the two systems made a mistake.

## Is one of them wrong?

No, and treating it as a contest is the fastest way to misunderstand both.

They're two measurement conventions, and each has centuries of practice built on top of it that assumes its own measurement. Western astrology developed a body of interpretation around seasonal symbolism, where Aries means the start of spring. Vedic astrology developed one around the actual stars, where the nakshatras are real groups of them you can point at.

Asking which zodiac is correct is like asking whether Celsius or Fahrenheit is the real temperature. What you can't do is read one scale and apply the other's rules to it.

## Why a Vedic chart can't be built on tropical positions

This is the part that matters, and it's not a matter of preference.

Your janma nakshatra is the lunar mansion the Moon occupied at birth, and nakshatras are divisions of the sidereal zodiac. Your Vimshottari dasha sequence starts from that nakshatra, so the entire timeline of mahadashas and antardashas, every start and end date, follows from a sidereal position. Feed it a tropical Moon and the sequence starts in the wrong nakshatra, which shifts your dasha periods by years.

Mangal dosha depends on which house Mars occupies counted from the Moon or the ascendant. Guna milan scores two Moon charts against each other. Both are sidereal calculations by construction.

So a tropical chart run through Vedic rules produces answers that look plausible, use the right vocabulary, and are quietly wrong by nearly a sign. That's worse than an obvious error, because nothing about the output announces it.

## The ayanamsa, and why the number isn't settled

Ayanamsa is simply the name for that 24 degree gap, and schools disagree about its exact size.

Lahiri is the Indian standard, adopted by the government's calendar reform committee, and it's what nearly all Indian astrologers work with. Raman and Krishnamurti sit within about a degree of it.

A degree sounds like nothing. It isn't. A graha sitting near a nakshatra boundary can cross into the next one under a different ayanamsa, which changes its pada and can move a dasha start date by months. So when a chart you got elsewhere disagrees with yours by a fraction of a degree, the ayanamsa is usually the reason rather than a mistake by either astrologer.

Anyone who tells you the ayanamsa question is closed is overselling. It's a real disagreement among people who've thought about it carefully.

## Where the precision actually comes from

The arithmetic here is astronomy, not interpretation, and the distinction is worth being precise about.

Planetary positions for a given instant and place are computed by the Swiss Ephemeris, the same high-precision library professional astrology software is built on. Sidereal longitudes come from subtracting the Lahiri ayanamsa from those positions. There's no estimation in that chain, and no AI anywhere in it. Where AI is used on RashiKundli, it reads a chart that has already been calculated and explains what the placements traditionally mean, in plain language. The calculation is deterministic and the explanation is written. Those are different jobs and it matters that they stay separate.

Manual calculation is where errors used to enter, and they were rarely dramatic. A missed minute of birth time, an ayanamsa applied for the wrong year, a transposed digit in a longitude. None of those announce themselves in the finished chart either.

## What none of this changes

Your birth moment didn't move. The sky was where it was, and both zodiacs are describing it accurately on their own terms.

What changes is which rules you can apply. If you're reading nakshatras, dashas, doshas or matching, you need the sidereal chart. If you're reading a Western transit column, you need the tropical one. Astrology is a traditional, faith-based practice, and this post is about the measurement underneath it rather than about what any placement predicts. Take decisions about health, money or the law to someone qualified in those things.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my Vedic sign different from my Western sign?

Because the two zodiacs start at different points and have drifted about 24 degrees apart since they last agreed. Most people's Sun moves back one sign. If you were born in the final few days of a tropical sign, it often doesn't move at all.

### Which zodiac should I use?

Whichever the rules you're applying were built for. Nakshatras, Vimshottari dasha, mangal dosha and guna milan are all sidereal. Western transit and progression work is tropical. Mixing them produces confident nonsense.

### Is sidereal astrology the same as Vedic astrology?

Effectively, yes. Vedic astrology, Jyotish and sidereal astrology all use the star-based zodiac, so a sidereal birth chart and a janam kundali are one thing described in different vocabularies.

### What exactly is the ayanamsa?

The angular difference between the two zodiacs at a given moment. In 2026 the Lahiri value is about 24 degrees and 13 minutes, and it grows by roughly 50 arcseconds a year.

### Does my ascendant change too?

Yes, and by the same amount, so the whole house structure shifts. That's why a sidereal chart often puts planets in different houses than a Western chart of the same birth, even before the two traditions disagree about how to divide houses at all.

### Can I just subtract 24 degrees myself?

For a rough idea, yes. For anything you intend to rely on, no, because the ayanamsa for your birth year isn't the same as this year's and the arithmetic has to be done per planet at full precision. That's what the calculator is for.
